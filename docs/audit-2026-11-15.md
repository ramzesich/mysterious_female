# Audit: what is seen, and what the decks hold (2026-11-15)

The user, before going on with the dash's rework (`docs/brainstorm/dash-rework.md`): "In order to
understand what super-power would be most fitting, we need to make sure the game is fully
playable, enjoyable, and can be finished without any superpower. I suggest running an audit": a
visual one (overlaps; placed things covered by background art; every text, idea or hint popup on
top of everything, unless the hull should hide it with its bearer), and one of the corridors (not
empty; an incentive to explore each to its end; a variety of such incentives, which may affect
progress). "Don't implement anything, write all your findings and suggestions separately and save
them."

And while it ran: "I have just run through the deck 4, and it's almost completely empty. I feel
like this is a major design flaw. There's nothing to do except running for a long gameplay time.
Jumping over one vacuum and one cart pusher. No items to collect, no fun obstacles."

**Nothing in the game was changed.** This file is the findings and the suggestions. The audit's
scripts are scratch files (`%TEMP%\bumbusik-plan\audit\`); §5 says which would be worth keeping.

## 1. How it was checked

| Check | What it does | Size |

| Occlusion by pairs (new, scratch) | Every picture drawn over another, pixel by pixel: how much of the one behind is covered | About 2,800 pictures a state, four states; 552 pairs; 48 where the one behind is a placed thing |
| **Visibility test (new, scratch)** | She is put beside each placed thing, the game is held still, the picture is taken with the thing and without it, and the changed pixels are counted against the thing's own. | Popups (new, scratch) | The same, for her own word over her head at every named place and in every room, and for the people's pictogram bubbles; and whether each is wholly inside the picture | 58 places and rooms, twice; 8 of the 13 bubbles |
| By eye | Close-ups of everything the tests named; contact sheets along ten stretches of deck | 21 close-ups, 53 tiles |
| The corridors, from the data | Everything placed along each deck, the gaps, what stands at each end (`maps/world.json`, `tools/ship_layout.py`, `docs/population.md`) | All ten decks and the outside |

**Limits.** I see stills, not motion. The visibility test did not go into the twenty small rooms
(their contents are few and drawn with the room). Things that move (the vacuum, the porter, the
waiter, the queue, the steward on his rounds, gulls) had walked off by the time they were tested:
their "hidden" readings are false and are listed as such. Popups were measured standing still, not
during scenes. Five bubbles could not be made to show in the two states used (the stage manager's,
the kid's, her husband's, the balcony guest's, the engineer's at the horn). **The whole playtest
suite was not run** (the standing rule); no scenario was run at all, since nothing was changed.

**What was measured.** The working folder changed branch under the audit: another session was at
work in it (the controller, then `cabin-washout`). The tests ran on what was checked out at the
time: the world's data and the placing of things are the same on those branches as on
`the-build`; the controller, the wider dim and room pictures being redrawn are not what was
measured. Two browser jobs may have run at once for a while; the readings are counts of pixels
with the game held still, so they do not depend on timing.

### 3.2 Findings

**C1. Region 1 is the emptiest part of the game, and it is the first (the user's own finding on
deck 4, confirmed by the data).**

- **Deck 4 on a first visit:** one tangerine on the whole deck's floor, and it is at the forward
  dead end (31 m), 36 m past the gangway she is heading for. From the service ladder where she
  comes up (95.6 m) aft to the stern (254 m) there are 158 m, nearly a minute of running, with the
  vacuum, two standing carts, one man in a bathrobe and nothing to pick up.
- **Its three rooms are shut or empty then:** the laundry and the dry store need the Housekeeping
  card (region 4); the stage manager's cabin has nothing in it until region 3.
- **Deck 5 is little better:** two tangerines on the open floor, the waiter (a hazard only with a
  tray), one cart; its forward cabins (20-74 m, 54 m) hold a cart, a steward knocking at a door
  and the trunk, and no reward at all. Shore excursions (110-150 m) is forty metres with one
  clerk at a desk.
- **The lunch errand then sends her the length of deck 4 or deck 5 again**, with a tray, at a
  walk.
- The plan asked for this (`docs/population.md` §4: "region 1 one moving thing and one breakable
  per stretch", "calm"). Built, calm reads as empty: the first half hour is the game's thinnest.

**C2. Every cabin corridor looks the same.** Decks 4, 5, 8, 9 and 10 are one wallpaper, one door,

## 4. Suggestions

Nothing here is decided. They are sorted by what they answer.

### 4.1 Something to pick up while she runs (answers C1, C4, C6, C9)

The game has one pickup, and 53 of them on 2,000 m of deck. Every platformer answers "running
through an empty corridor" the same way: a small common pickup laid in lines and arcs, so that
running and jumping well is itself the game.

- **I1. Bumbot's paw prints (recommended).** He is the stowaway and the story already says he is
  "literally the map". Prints on the floor, up a wall, along a ledge, over a cart, into a vent:
  she notes each by running over it (a soft sound, a count on the HUD by deck). Nothing floats: a
  print is always on a surface. A line of them pulls her down a corridor to its end and up onto
  things; where a line stops, something is there (a tangerine, a keepsake, a peek, his next
  hiding place). Each deck's full set could pay out once (I8).
  Cost: one small sprite in a few turns (the game has paw prints already, by the pool), a
  counter, placing. No generations needed.
- **I2. More tangerines on the open floor of decks 4, 5 and 6**, up on things that ask for a jump
  (a cart's top to a hose cabinet, a stack of crates to a shelf). The user's "keep the current
  tangerines where we are" was about not moving the 53; adding some is theirs to allow. Fits the
  rework's "a tangerine forgives a hit": the first region is where a beginner needs them.

### 4.2 More kinds of thing in the way, in the first region (answers C1)

The rule "no hazard twice" stands, so deck 4 and 5 need their own. Deck 4 is embarkation day and
the service deck; deck 5 is the morning.

- **I3. Embarkation's bustle on deck 4:** a family coming out of a cabin with a stroller (wait, or
  hop the stroller's front); a runaway suitcase on wheels that rolls with the ship's list (a
  timed thing to jump); a dog on a long lead across the corridor (hop the lead; it barks at the
  vacuum); a floor polisher whose cable snakes across the carpet (jump the cable as it whips).
- **I4. A second lane on the service deck:** deck 4's ceiling carries pipes and cable trays. A few
  runs of them low enough to stand on, reached from a cart or a crate, with prints and a
  tangerine along them: the corridor gets a height, and the jump gets a use before deck 6.
- **I5. The rooms open earlier.** The laundry or the dry store without a card in region 1 (the
  green lock moved to a cupboard inside), so that deck 4 has a door that opens on a first visit.

### 4.3 A reason to reach each end (answers C3, C5)

A variety, so that no two ends pay the same way:

- **I6. A keepsake at the end** (the second collection of `docs/brainstorm/dash-rework.md`,
  still to be chosen there: things for Bumbot, or his loot). The plainest fit for the nine empty
  ends of C3 and behind the boat drill.
- **I7. A look-out.** The forward corridors of decks 5, 8 and 9 end at the superstructure's
  front; the Stardust balcony ends over the stage; the Great Outdoors ends over the wake. At
  each, a window or a rail where standing still eases the camera out to a wide view (the intro
  already does this: the ship from afar, one clock). The first time, the view is kept as a
  postcard. Cost: code, and a porthole or a window piece for the corridor ends.
- **I8. Rounds.** The gangway officer's Deck card makes her "doing his rounds now": real ships
  have a watchman's point at the far end of every corridor. A small reader at each of the
  thirteen cabin corridors' ends; E clocks it; the HUD's deck strip ticks. **This is the one that
  could touch progress:** a deck whose rounds are done opens its fire doors from both sides (the
  one-way doors of decks 8, 9 and 10 become shortcuts), or the officer pays out at five, ten and
  all. Going to the end once makes every later walk shorter.
- **I9. A delivery by cabin number.** A room-service tray waits outside a pantry with a tag that
  reads a cabin number (a label, not a hint); the cabin is at a far end; under tray rules the
  hallway's hazards become a route to choose. A hand takes the tray; thanks. Three or four, each a
  different load (a tray, an ice bucket, flowers, a cake for the party cabin on deck 8). **This
  gives the optional hallways of C4 a purpose**, and uses what is built: carrying, the number
  plaques, the pantries.
- **I10. A peek or a gag** at the ends that stay small: the cabin door ajar with a guest at the
  window (deck 5, planned and not built), a snorer, a cloche she can lift (under it: a shoe, a
  tangerine, a paw print in butter), a picture hanging crooked that she straightens, a mirror
  (the hair clip exists).
- **I11. A way through, so an end is not a turn-back:** a crew ladder at a corridor's far end up
  or down to the next deck's far end (deck 5 forward to deck 6 forward; deck 9 aft to deck 10
  aft). The trunks are of this kind already. It touches the ship's layout, so it is the user's to
  allow ("Don't change the ship's basic layout").
- **I12. His traces.** Where a line of prints ends without him: a vase knocked over, a tangerine
  peel, a tail going into a vent. A beat of story for the price of a prop.

### 4.4 Each deck its own look (answers C2)

- **I13. Build what the plan had:** the plaques' coloured band per deck, and each deck's own
  pictures. Pictures by PixelLab would be about ten generations for a sheet, or drawn in code;
  the wallpaper's tint per deck costs nothing.
- **I14. One thing per stretch found nowhere else,** as the plan's rule said (deck 4 midships
  the laundry's steam through a door, deck 5 forward the smell of breakfast as trays at every
  door, deck 9 forward gold-framed portraits of captains).

### 4.5 The visual faults

- V1: draw the note's text over its board (or drop the text: the board's art has a cat on it).
- V2: move the wet-floor sign a metre off the lift's door.
- V3: move the reader or the notice board on decks 9 and 10.
- V4: the Spinnaker's balcony needs a decision: draw her and the balcony's things in front of the
  windscreen's glass (the bulwark still covers her feet), or keep the balcony bare and short.
- V5: the promenade's enclosed ends need the same decision: cut the side open there while she is
  in them, as a deck's is.
- V6 to V8: the bag moved clear of the sunbed; the three signs moved off where people stand; a
  thin dark outline on her word's box.

### 4.6 A suggested order

1. The five real visual faults (V1 to V5): small, and V4 and V5 are places where she cannot be
   seen.
2. Region 1 (C1): prints (I1), two or three of deck 4's own obstacles (I3), a room that opens
   (I5). It is the first thing she will play.
3. The ends (C3) with a mix of I6, I7 and I10, and the rounds (I8) if the user wants ends to
   count for progress.
4. The optional hallways (C4) with deliveries (I9).
5. The look of each deck (I13).

Then the superpower question of `docs/brainstorm/dash-rework.md` can be asked again of a game
that plays well without one.

## 5. Tools worth keeping

The visibility test caught what nothing else could (a thing hidden by a drawn shape or the ship's
side) and took about twenty minutes for 600 things. As `tools/check_visible.py`, with its list of
movers and of things meant, it would be a standing check beside `tools/check_overlaps.py`. The
popup test is a few minutes and could join it. Not added to the repo now: the user asked for
findings only.
